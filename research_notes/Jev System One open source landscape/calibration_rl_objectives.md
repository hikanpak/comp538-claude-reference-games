# Calibration-aware RL objectives for language-model decisions: TypeSafe's RLCD, its open reproductions, and the academic methods

Scope and conventions. Current as of 1 November 2026. Every fact about Jev and RLCD below comes from a page fetched in this session, with its URL. Commentator claims are kept apart from TypeSafe's own statements. Claims about academic results carry one of four status labels: Proven, Heuristic, Empirical, Open.

Name collision. "RLCD" also names Reinforcement Learning from Contrastive Distillation (Kevin Yang, Dan Klein, Asli Celikyilmaz, Nanyun Peng, Yuandong Tian, arXiv 2307.12950), a preference-pair method that aligns a model to natural-language principles without human feedback and outperforms RLAIF and context distillation on harmlessness, helpfulness and story outlines — [arXiv 2307.12950v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12950v2). Its ICLR 2024 venue is given by a community wiki that I did not confirm against the conference page — [search result summary, HF rl-llm-wiki discussion](https://huggingface.co/datasets/rl-llm-wiki/knowledge-base/discussions/150). It is unrelated to TypeSafe's RLCD and plays no further part in these notes. A third use exists: Cloudflare names a separate training stage "RLCD" in its Clef launch post of 1 October 2026, which systemonemodels.org says does not match TypeSafe's method — [systemonemodels.org RLCD guide](https://systemonemodels.org/guides/rlcd-explained/).

## 1. What has TypeSafe said about RLCD, and what have commentators inferred?

### Takeaway
TypeSafe has published the name, the goal (decisions with "epistemically honest probabilities") and the output contract, but no reward, loss, data or algorithm. The claim that RLCD uses proper scoring rules such as the Brier score as rewards is commentator inference (Towards AI, dsebastien.net's cited guesses, an arXiv pentest paper), never a TypeSafe statement.

### Cited Findings

TypeSafe's own statements (primary sources)
- The launch post, "Introducing System One Models & Jev", by Diogo Almeida (founder), is dated Sep 15, 2026 in its header (page metadata says Oct 28, 2026). It calls RLCD "a training method we call Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions (RLCD)" and states its optimization target as "Calibrated decisions: answers with epistemically honest probabilities on System One tasks." — [TypeSafe blog](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)
- The same post sets RLCD against RLHF, which optimizes "Human preference: writeups and chat responses that human raters prefer", and RLVR, which optimizes "Verifiable rewards: outputs that can be programmatically verified." — [TypeSafe blog](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)
- The post claims Jev is "Calibrated: higher confidence means higher accuracy", returns "Type-safe structured values" whose possible outputs are defined in advance, accompanies all answers "with calibrated probabilities and confidence scores", and "outputs all probabilities in parallel instead of autoregressively generating by token." It names no reward, no scoring rule, and no calibration metric such as ECE or Brier, and defers the reliability argument "for reasons we will get into in the future." — [TypeSafe blog](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)
- The post claims 70 ms to 500 ms end-to-end latency, "40x-200x faster" on System One-shaped queries, and handles high-cardinality choices (up to 255 options) with "a two-stage system of scoring independently then making an explicit choice." It does not describe deferral to a larger model. — [TypeSafe blog](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)
- TypeSafe's ML primer says RLCD "trains TypeSafe to return decisions and calibrated probabilities instead of generated text", defines calibration as a 0.2 probability coming true about 20% of the time across many predictions, and says these rates describe groups of predictions, not any single answer. It mentions no reward, scoring rule or synthetic data, and criticizes RLHF for rewarding sycophancy, confident-sounding hallucinations and mode dropping. — [TypeSafe docs, ML primer](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction/machine-learning-primer)
- TypeSafe's Confidence page separates the `probabilities` vector from a scalar `confidence` computed from its shape: the distance of the yes-probability from 0.5 for yes/no ("Noul") questions, and the top probability rescaled against the uniform value for Choice questions. It proposes three bands (act automatically, ask for confirmation, route elsewhere), uses 0.5 and 0.9 thresholds in example code, says "The correct threshold values depend on your domain", and mentions fallback to "a different system" but not to an LLM. It publishes no calibration metric. — [TypeSafe docs, Confidence](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence)
- systemonemodels.org records that TypeSafe writes "for" while TechCrunch (18 September 2026) quotes Almeida saying "from", and attributes to TypeSafe and TechCrunch the claim that Jev was trained on synthetic data. — [systemonemodels.org RLCD guide](https://systemonemodels.org/guides/rlcd-explained/)
- dsebastien.net quotes Almeida on the Latent Space podcast describing RLCD as a new task framing rather than a specific algorithm ("RLCD is this new task..."), and records his admission that the technique is "novel, unpublished." — [dsebastien.net](https://www.dsebastien.net/reinforcement-learning-for-calibrated-decisions-rlcd/)

Commentator interpretation
- Towards AI ("RLCD: Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions") says RLCD replaces binary (+1/0) rewards with proper scoring rules such as the Brier score plus a batch calibration penalty, and casts Jev as an actor network that outputs a decision with a calibrated probability distribution. I could only read the search snippet because the article returned HTTP 403, and the article gives no TypeSafe source for these claims. — [Towards AI (snippet only)](https://pub.towardsai.net/rlcd-reinforcement-learning-for-calibrated-decisions-e528daf3591d)
- dsebastien.net (Sébastien Dubois, 23 Sep 2026) lists the scoring-rule account explicitly as guesses: a strictly proper scoring rule (log loss or Brier) on the output distribution, proposed by Archer Hume after about 10,000 API calls, and a "schema-conditioned Plackett-Luce objective" plus a Brier calibration term, proposed by Di Zhang. The author also questions whether "RL" is the right label, since supervised cross-entropy suffices when outcomes are known. — [dsebastien.net](https://www.dsebastien.net/reinforcement-learning-for-calibrated-decisions-rlcd/)
- The same page reports independent black-box measurements of Jev: ECE 0.031 on MMLU (Hume) and ECE 0.107 on synthetic support tickets (Beri), and cites Maio's critique that individually calibrated judgments "do not automatically compose" into calibrated workflows. It contrasts RLCR and Rewarding Doubt, which calibrate a number inside generated text, with Jev, which appears to calibrate its native output distribution. — [dsebastien.net](https://www.dsebastien.net/reinforcement-learning-for-calibrated-decisions-rlcd/)
- Envisioning defines RLCD as optimizing "a model's predicted probabilities to match real-world outcome frequencies", states that the "loss function, reward model, and calibration procedure remain undisclosed", and notes that RLCD has been described only in TypeSafe's blog post and press coverage. — [Envisioning](https://www.envisioning.com/vocab/rlcd-reinforcement-learning-for-calibrated-decisions)
- systemonemodels.org says TypeSafe has published "the name and the goal, not the method", with "no dataset, no training code, no step count", and that an arXiv search for the exact phrase on 20 September 2026 returned zero results. Its only Brier reference is the metadata tag of a Hugging Face model (`heman10x/rlcd-modernbert-151m`), which it calls a lead, not a reproduction. — [systemonemodels.org RLCD guide](https://systemonemodels.org/guides/rlcd-explained/)
- OrcaRouter's "RLCD explained" post (Spanish version found, English URL returned only a title) presents RLCD as the third method after RLHF and RLVR, optimizing for honest stated probabilities, and attributes the framing to TypeSafe's launch material. — [OrcaRouter (es)](https://www.orcarouter.ai/es/blog/rlcd-explained)
- Barbosa (arXiv 2609.28940, 24 Sep 2026) proposes "lightweight non-generative classifiers" returning "calibrated verdicts" as System One decision layers in pentest harnesses, at four decision points (finding adjudication, severity recalibration, agent pruning, confirmation loops), and cautions that benchmark results for Jev should not be assumed to transfer to pentesting. The abstract defines no training objective. — [arXiv 2609.28940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28940)
- The Li, Miao, Krishnan and Padman survey (CMU, 21 Sep 2026) concludes that TypeSafe's public materials "establish that design direction, while leaving the full training recipe insufficiently specified for identification with an open academic algorithm", and warns that the `confidence` statistic is not a correctness probability until validated. — [Li et al. survey PDF](https://yubol-bobo.github.io/rlcd-survey/pdf/calibration-aware-rl-survey.pdf)

### Inferences
- The proper-scoring-rule reading is plausible because it is the textbook way to make a stated probability "honest", but it is an inference from the stated goal. Nothing TypeSafe has published rules out a supervised log-loss objective, which is itself a proper scoring rule applied to a fixed label.
- Almeida's "new task framing" remark suggests RLCD may name the problem setting (typed, bounded decisions scored on probability quality) more than a specific optimizer, which supports the course project defining its own objective rather than reconstructing TypeSafe's.

### Gaps
- The full Towards AI text (HTTP 403) and the TechCrunch article were not read directly. Pages found but not fetched: saulius.io, befailproof.ai, sanity.io glossary, utkarsh-ankit.github.io/RLCD, mindstudio.ai, hackernoon.
- TypeSafe has published no calibration numbers for Jev. The only figures are third-party black-box measurements reported secondhand on dsebastien.net.

## 2. Which community or open-source projects implement an RLCD-style objective, what reward or loss do they use, and what do they report?

### Takeaway
No project reproduces TypeSafe's RLCD, because the recipe is unpublished. Four projects do implement a scoring-rule RL objective in the open (OpenJev-RLCD, rlcd-lite, open-rlcd, decider v10). Their shared finding is that a scoring-rule reward fixes RLVR's overconfidence but rarely beats supervised training followed by temperature scaling. JEV-27B is distillation, LitJev is training-free, and "OpenSysOne" could not be found.

### Cited Findings

Projects that train with a proper-scoring-rule RL objective
- OpenJev-RLCD (Zhimin Gao, Pichao Wang, arXiv 2609.38850, 30 Sep 2026) samples a rationale, reads the answer distribution committed after it, and scores each rationale's own distribution with a strictly proper rule, mainly Brier (log also tested). It shows that scoring the mixture of several rationales equals the average per-rationale score plus a disagreement bonus, which rewards randomness, and argues that RLVR is the mixture objective without that diversity term. — [arXiv 2609.38850](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38850v1)
- OpenJev-RLCD reports two failure modes of end-to-end training: collapse into "System-One" behavior with empty rationales, and swamping by high-variance policy-gradient noise. Its recipe is "calibrate, then reinforce": stage 1 trains the post-rationale decision with the proper score using pathwise gradients only, and stage 2 reinforces the reasoning with the same score as reward, a down-weighted score-function term (c = 0.3) and a KL anchor (β = 0.04) to the stage-1 model, using leave-one-out REINFORCE rather than PPO. — [arXiv 2609.38850](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38850v1)
- On Qwen3-1.7B with three seeds and temperature scaling for all arms, OpenJev-RLCD reports GSM8K-Verify accuracy 92.2% vs 91.8% (GRPO+KL), Brier 0.135 vs 0.150, and coverage at 5% error 81.1% vs 19.4%. On MMLU-Pro it reports accuracy 47.7% vs 42.4% and coverage at 20% error 27.6% vs 15.6% (SFT+TS) and 0% for reasoning baselines. Stage 2 helped on GSM8K-Verify and was neutral on MMLU-Pro, and the authors cannot yet predict when it helps. — [arXiv 2609.38850](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38850v1)
- rlcd-lite (arnabgho) calls itself "a reconstruction from the public description of Jev", "not TypeSafe's algorithm." It uses parallel constrained decoding (one fused forward pass with a block-diagonal mask, one masked step per field, softmax over single-token option labels, confidence restricted to buckets 1–5) and GRPO on Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct with LoRA r=16. Its default reward is RLCR-style: correctness indicator minus squared error between confidence and correctness. — [rlcd-lite GitHub](https://github.com/arnabgho/rlcd-lite)
- rlcd-lite reports on synthetic 3-field ticket triage: base accuracy 0.49, stated ECE 0.395; binary GRPO accuracy 0.76, stated ECE 0.352; Brier-only GRPO accuracy 0.21, stated ECE 0.086; RLCR-style GRPO accuracy 0.78, stated ECE 0.084. The author's findings: Brier on stated confidence alone "is a trap" because the policy learns to be predictably wrong with low confidence; RL pushes raw softmax to 0.99–1.00 mean confidence and temperature scaling repairs much of this (RLCR softmax ECE 0.211 to 0.125); the stated confidence has near-zero resolution; a `needs_human` field is never learned at 0.5B; reshuffling option order flips about half of base decisions on BoolQ and MMLU. — [rlcd-lite GitHub](https://github.com/arnabgho/rlcd-lite)
- open-rlcd (Louiskq), "one reading of RLCD", rewards a model with "a proper scoring rule applied to the probabilities it states over typed options" through an importance-weighted estimator. Reported ECE: toy task RLVR 0.381, RLCD 0.011, supervised 0.009; 9B model on the dev split 0.030 / 0.021 / 0.015, and after one fitted temperature 0.021 / 0.023 / 0.019; harder split 0.058 / 0.026 / 0.027. In Breakout, where outcomes depend on the model's own moves and the author argues RL is actually needed, belief log loss is RLVR 0.979, RLCD 0.488, supervised 0.592 on one seed. The author concludes RLCD fixes RLVR's overconfidence but gives no clear advantage over supervised training plus temperature scaling. No trained model is published. — [open-rlcd GitHub](https://github.com/Louiskq/open-rlcd)
- decider (Mapika) fine-tunes Qwen3.5-2B/4B/35B-A3B base models with cross-entropy on about 95 public datasets plus labels from a local Qwen3.5-27B teacher. decider-2b v10 is "The RL stage that turns v8 into v10", where for actions in games with a known probability law the model's answer "is scored against the exact law with a log score." The RL stage needs MiniWoB++ in a live Chrome and is not in the package. — [decider GitHub](https://github.com/Mapika/decider)
- decider reports that v10 sits 0.22 nats above the exact law against 0.47 for v8, improves browser-click log score from −0.35 to −0.03, and has ECE on hard items of 0.31 (v10), 0.18 (v11) and 0.10 (decider-4b v2). Per-type temperatures are fitted by NLL with `decider.calibrate`. — [decider GitHub](https://github.com/Mapika/decider)
- SiliconLabAI/OpenJev is a TypeScript playground with three backends (per-option LLM call plus softmax, one structured JSON call, or a decider server) and lists `decider-2b` v10 as including calibration-aware RL. It publishes no reward or metric. — [OpenJev GitHub](https://github.com/SiliconLabAI/OpenJev)

Projects named in the brief that do not use RL
- JEV-27B (AutoTrust AI, Apache-2.0) freezes a Qwen3.8-27B backbone and trains a LoRA System 1 block (about 108.9M parameters) and a 24-slot decision head by distillation from TypeSafe Jev 1.13's output distributions, with loss KL(target ‖ model) plus 0.5 times a ranked probability score for 0–5 score questions, on about 741k rows. It self-reports ECE 0.0009 and Brier 0.0013 (both measured against the teacher), Noul AUROC 0.995, KL 0.104 on unseen task families, and a six-benchmark mean of 84.07% vs 83.85% for Jev 1.13. It states it is "not affiliated with, endorsed by, or a product of TypeSafe AI." — [JEV-27B model card](https://huggingface.co/autotrust/JEV-27B)
- A third-party listing adds that JEV-27B's calibration is verified only inside its 53 training domains and degrades on unseen task families. I saw this only as a search summary. — [nocode.mba release tracker (search summary)](https://www.nocode.mba/ai-release-tracker/releases/autotrust-jev-27b)
- LitJev (zhengxuyu) is "a hypothesis-based reproduction from public information" of Jev's decision layer that needs "No training": it sums each option's token log-probabilities on a Qwen checkpoint (default Qwen3.8-27B) and softmaxes, with probabilities "not calibrated by default" and optional post-hoc temperature fitting. It reports no calibration figures and does not mention RLCD. — [LitJev GitHub](https://github.com/zhengxuyu/litjev)

The wider landscape
- systemonemodels.org lists about 38 alternatives and concludes "RLCD has never been described in enough detail to copy, so nothing here reproduces it." Calibrated entries fit a temperature after training: Laya (ECE 0.466 to 0.081 after temperature), Kev-9B (Brier 0.237 on a new-source test set), Von (ECE 0.107), open-alternative-jev (5.4% to 2.1% on MMLU). Cloudflare's Clef is listed as Brier loss plus "Cloudflare's RLCD stage" with ECE 0.040 (Clef) and 0.025 (Clef-flash) on the Jev Decision Index, which I did not verify on a Cloudflare page. — [systemonemodels.org alternatives](https://systemonemodels.org/examples/alternatives/)
- Hugging Face models with "rlcd" in the name created 16–19 September 2026 (`harshatheg/Qwen-2.5-1B-RLCD`, `notnotsamuel/LFM2.5-350M-RLCD`, `monotykamary/LFM2.5-2.6B-RLCD`, `anthonym21/qwen3-0.6b-rlcd-decision`, `larkooo/gemma-e2b-rlcd`) document no reproduction, and `TaylorAI/Llama-3B-RLCD-SFT` predates Jev and refers to contrastive distillation. — [systemonemodels.org RLCD guide](https://systemonemodels.org/guides/rlcd-explained/)
- The Li et al. survey inspects eight community artifacts and says "their names do not establish a reproduction of TypeSafe's RL method"; AlexWortega/openjev, for example, is a supervised sequence classifier trained with cross-entropy. — [Li et al. survey PDF](https://yubol-bobo.github.io/rlcd-survey/pdf/calibration-aware-rl-survey.pdf)
- Roitman's "Hitchhiker's Guide to System 1 Models" (arXiv 2610.33775v2, 29 Oct 2026) has sections on RLCD (6.2.2, 10.3), RLCR (6.2.3), OpenJev-RLCD (6.6), calibration metrics (9.1) and System 1 + System 2 cascades with confidence-based routing (13.1). A search summary claimed the guide says an open RLCD reproduction used SFT with temperature scaling instead of PPO with Brier rewards; the first 100k characters I read did not contain that claim, so it is unverified. — [arXiv 2610.33775](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.33775)

### Inferences
- The brief's premise that JEV-27B is an RLCD-style RL reproduction does not hold: it is a KL distillation student of Jev, so its tiny ECE measures agreement with the teacher, not calibration against ground truth.
- Across open-rlcd, rlcd-lite and the systemonemodels survey of projects, supervised proper-score training plus one fitted temperature is the baseline to beat. RL earns its place when outcomes depend on the model's own actions (open-rlcd Breakout, decider v10 games and browser clicks), which is the agent-harness case.
- rlcd-lite's "Brier trap" and OpenJev-RLCD's mixture identity point to the same design rule: when the model chooses the event it reports on, the reward must include a correctness or utility term, or the policy can satisfy calibration by being reliably wrong.

### Gaps
- "OpenSysOne" and its reproduction guide returned no results under several queries. It may be a misremembered name for OpenJev or OpenJev-RLCD.
- decider v10's full RL reward and algorithm are not published in the repository.
- None of the RL reproductions is peer reviewed, and each reports one model scale with few seeds.

## 3. Academic methods that train language models so that stated confidence matches accuracy

### Takeaway
The academic line runs from the observation that RLHF degrades calibration (GPT-4 report, Kadavath et al.) through elicitation and supervised calibration-tuning to RL with proper-scoring-rule rewards (RLCR, Rewarding Doubt) and abstention-aware rewards (TruthRL, behaviorally calibrated RL). RLCR's reward, binary correctness plus a Brier term, is the closest published analogue of what commentators guess RLCD to be.

### Cited Findings

RLHF and calibration
- GPT-4 Technical Report (OpenAI, arXiv 2303.08774): "the pre-trained model is highly calibrated" but "after the post-training process, the calibration is reduced (Figure 8)." Figure 8 plots answer-option logprob calibration on an MMLU subset with ECE 0.007 for the pre-trained model and 0.074 for the PPO model, captioned "The post-training hurts calibration significantly." Empirical, MMLU subset. — [arXiv 2303.08774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774v2) (text extracted from the v6 PDF)
- Kadavath, Conerly, Askell et al. (36 authors), "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know", arXiv 2207.05221 (2022). Larger pretrained models are well calibrated on multiple-choice and true/false questions when formatted properly. Self-evaluation as P(True) of a proposed answer works and improves when the model sees several of its own samples, and a trained P(IK) head ("I know") partly transfers across tasks but is poorly calibrated on new ones. Section 3.3 shows RLHF policies "naively seem miscalibrated, but with a simple temperature adjustment they become fairly well-calibrated" (Figure 9 compares T=1 with T=2.5). Empirical, 52B Anthropic models. — [arXiv 2207.05221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221)
- Leng, Huang, Zhu, Huang, "Taming Overconfidence in LLMs: Reward Calibration in RLHF", arXiv 2410.09724. RLHF makes models verbally overconfident, partly because PPO reward models favor high-confidence responses regardless of quality. PPO-M adds explicit confidence scores to reward-model training, and PPO-C adjusts the reward during PPO by its gap to a moving average of past rewards. Both reduce calibration error at comparable task performance on Llama3-8B and Mistral-7B across six datasets. Empirical. — [arXiv 2410.09724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09724)
- Bereket and Leskovec, "Uncalibrated Reasoning: GRPO Induces Overconfidence for Stochastic Outcomes", arXiv 2508.11800. On synthetic and real biological binary outcomes, GRPO yields overconfident probabilities while PPO and RLOO stay calibrated, and "removing group standard normalization in GRPO fixes its miscalibration", with a theoretical account. Empirical with supporting theory. — [arXiv 2508.11800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11800)

Verbalized confidence (elicitation, no training)
- Tian, Mitchell, Zhou, Sharma, Rafailov, Yao, Finn, Manning, "Just Ask for Calibration", EMNLP 2023, arXiv 2305.14975. For RLHF models (ChatGPT, GPT-4, Claude) on TriviaQA, SciQ and TruthfulQA, verbalized confidence is typically better calibrated than conditional token probabilities, often cutting ECE by a relative 50%. Empirical. — [arXiv 2305.14975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975)
- Xiong, Hu, Lu, Li, Fu, He, Hooi, "Can LLMs Express Their Uncertainty?", ICLR 2024, arXiv 2306.13063. A black-box framework of prompting, sampling and consistency aggregation finds verbalized confidence overconfident, partly mitigated by human-inspired prompts and sample consistency, and white-box methods only slightly better (failure-prediction AUROC 0.522 to 0.605). Empirical, five datasets, five LLMs. — [arXiv 2306.13063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063)

Supervised calibration-tuning
- Kapoor, Gruver, Roberts, Collins, Pal, Bhatt, Weller, Dooley, Goldblum, Wilson, "Large Language Models Must Be Taught to Know What They Don't Know", NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2406.08391. Prompting alone does not give calibrated uncertainty. Fine-tuning through the model's features with LoRA on graded correct/incorrect answers works, "a thousand graded examples are sufficient to outperform baseline methods", the estimator generalizes, and models can estimate other models' uncertainty. Empirical. — [arXiv 2406.08391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08391)

RL with proper-scoring-rule rewards on confidence
- Damani, Puri, Slocum, Shenfeld, Choshen, Kim, Andreas, "Beyond Binary Rewards: Training LMs to Reason About Their Uncertainty" (RLCR), arXiv 2507.16806, ICLR 2026. The model reasons, answers, and verbalizes a numerical confidence. The reward is binary correctness plus the negative squared error between confidence and correctness (Brier). — [arXiv 2507.16806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16806); venue — [ML Anthology ICLR 2026](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2026/damani2026iclr-beyond/)
- RLCR theory: any bounded proper scoring rule can replace the Brier term provided its gap between the correct and incorrect scores stays below the correctness bonus, so the optimum is both accurate and calibrated. The log score fails this because its gap is unbounded near zero confidence, so the reward can favor confidently wrong answers. Proven (in the paper). — [arXiv 2507.16806v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16806v2)
- RLCR setup and results: Qwen2.5-7B base, GRPO without KL and without std normalization (BNPO loss), on HotpotQA with distractor paragraphs removed (20k) and Big-Math (15k). HotpotQA in-domain: RLVR accuracy 63.0%, AUROC 0.50, Brier 0.37, ECE 0.37 versus RLCR 62.1%, 0.69, 0.21, 0.03. HotpotQA out-of-domain average: RLVR 53.9% and ECE 0.46 versus RLCR 56.2% and 0.21. Math in-domain: RLVR 72.9% and ECE 0.26 versus RLCR 72.7% and 0.10. Confidence-weighted majority vote beats plain majority vote averaged over 77 datasets. Empirical. — [arXiv 2507.16806v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16806v2)
- Bani-Harouni, Pellegrini, Stangel, Özsoy, Zaripova, Navab, Keicher, "Rewarding Doubt", arXiv 2503.02623 (preprint, v6 Feb 2026). RL fine-tuning with a reward based on the logarithmic scoring rule on verbalized confidence for factual QA, whose optimal policy is argued to be perfectly calibrated, with substantially better calibration and transfer to unseen tasks. Empirical (numbers not checked). — [arXiv 2503.02623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02623)
- Wu, Liu, Zeng, Zhan, Cai, Huang, "Mitigating LLM Hallucination via Behaviorally Calibrated Reinforcement Learning", arXiv 2512.19920 (Dec 2025). Binary RLVR encourages "guessing whenever correctness probability exceeds zero". Training with strictly proper scoring rules makes Qwen3-4B-Instruct output a calibrated correctness probability so it can abstain or flag uncertain claims, with a BeyondAIME log-scale accuracy-to-hallucination gain of 0.806 against GPT-5's 0.207. Empirical. — [arXiv 2512.19920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19920)
- The Li et al. survey shows that the bounded answer/abstain utilities in Wu et al. (+1 or −1 for an answered outcome, 2t − 1 for abstaining at threshold t), averaged over a uniform threshold distribution, give exactly correctness-plus-Brier, the RLCR reward. It also cites OpenForecaster's reward of twice correctness minus squared error, which cut near-zero-confidence "Unknown" answers from about 40% to 4%. Proven (as a derivation, specific to that utility scaling). — [Li et al. survey PDF](https://yubol-bobo.github.io/rlcd-survey/pdf/calibration-aware-rl-survey.pdf)

Rewarding abstention and "I don't know"
- Kalai, Nachum, Vempala, Zhang, "Why Language Models Hallucinate", arXiv 2509.04664 (Sep 2025). Binary grading gives abstention no credit, so guessing is never worse than "I don't know". Section 4.2 proposes stating a target in the prompt ("Answer only if you are >t confident") with mistakes penalized t/(1−t), correct answers +1 and IDK 0, so answering beats abstaining in expectation only when confidence exceeds t, and one behavior is optimal for all targets. The paper calls this "behavioral calibration." Proven (short expected-score argument). — [arXiv 2509.04664 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04664v1)
- Wei, Yang, Sun et al., "TruthRL: Incentivizing Truthful LLMs via Reinforcement Learning", ICML 2026, arXiv 2509.25760. A GRPO ternary reward separating correct answers, hallucinations and abstentions cuts hallucination from 43.5% to 19.4% and raises truthfulness from 5.3% to 37.2% across backbones. — [arXiv 2509.25760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25760). The Li et al. survey gives the values as +1, 0, −1, which make the ideal answer threshold 0.5, and notes that on CRAG with retrieval TruthRL reports accuracy 56.6% and hallucination 19.4% against 60.3% and 39.5% for binary RL, so it trades some correct answers for fewer errors and measures no probability calibration. Empirical. — [Li et al. survey PDF](https://yubol-bobo.github.io/rlcd-survey/pdf/calibration-aware-rl-survey.pdf)
- Che, Yuan, Zhao, Yu, "Abstention as an Action Can Kill Both the Reward Gradient and the KL Anchor", arXiv 2608.00301 (2026), as summarized by the survey: with a shared bounded answer gate under error-penalized rewards, both the task gradient and the KL gradient saturate, so a mostly low-success training population can suppress answering even on easy inputs. I did not fetch the paper itself. — [Li et al. survey PDF](https://yubol-bobo.github.io/rlcd-survey/pdf/calibration-aware-rl-survey.pdf)

Calibration in agentic RL (2026)
- Zhu, "Closing the Reflection Gap: A Free Calibration Bonus for Agentic RL" (RefGRPO), arXiv 2606.14211. Agents misjudge their outputs after environment feedback and standard RL barely fixes this because of a credit-assignment mismatch. A bonus contrasting the agent's reflection with the actual outcome, under a dynamic coefficient schedule, cuts underconfidence from 44.4% to 7.7% and raises text-to-SQL accuracy from 75.1% to 76.5%, and supports selective prediction. Empirical. — [arXiv 2606.14211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14211)
- Xuan, Zeng, Qi, Xiao, Wang, Yokoya, "The Confidence Dichotomy: Analyzing and Mitigating Miscalibration in Tool-Use Agents", arXiv 2601.07264. In multi-turn tool use, evidence tools such as web search "systematically induce severe overconfidence" while verification tools such as code interpreters reduce it. An RL fine-tuning framework that jointly optimizes accuracy and calibration generalizes to noisy web settings and math. Empirical. — [arXiv 2601.07264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07264)
- The Li et al. survey (CMU, 21 Sep 2026) organizes the field by probability target, objective, implemented update and decision use, and frames three divergences: reporting versus capability, stated objective versus implemented update (group normalization, token masks, finite rollouts), and ordering versus decision value. It cites further 2026 agentic work it summarizes but I did not fetch, including DualStake (arXiv 2609.00935) and UCPO (arXiv 2601.22648). — [Li et al. survey PDF](https://yubol-bobo.github.io/rlcd-survey/pdf/calibration-aware-rl-survey.pdf)

### Inferences
- The GPT-4 and Kadavath results concern token-level answer probabilities, the same readout a Jev-style model uses, so the RLHF calibration loss is the directly relevant failure. Kadavath's temperature fix suggests much of that loss is a global sharpening that one scalar repairs, consistent with rlcd-lite's observation.
- RLCR, Rewarding Doubt and TruthRL train a generated confidence token or an abstain action. A Jev-style model instead exposes a full distribution over a fixed option set, so the closest academic analogue for the action distribution itself is supervised proper-score training (log loss), with RL needed only when labels come from outcomes of the model's own actions.

### Gaps
- Not verified in this session, so not cited: R-Tuning (Zhang et al., NAACL 2024), SaySelf, LACIE, ConfTuner, linguistic calibration (Band et al., ICML 2024), AbstentionBench, and the Wen et al. abstention survey. Several appear in the Li et al. survey's references.
- Exact ECE and AUROC numbers for Rewarding Doubt, PPO-M/PPO-C and calibration-tuning were not on the abstract pages and were not read from the full papers.

## 4. How do these objectives interact with RL in a sequential setting where calibrated confidence decides when to defer to a larger model?

### Takeaway
Proper scoring rules guarantee calibration for a fixed event in expectation, and the classical defer rule (act when calibrated confidence exceeds a cost-derived threshold) follows directly. Sequential settings break both premises, because the model's actions change which events occur and deferral now trades against deferral later. Learning-to-defer, sequential learning-to-defer, conformal "ask for help" planners and LLM cascades supply the pieces, but none combines them with RL-trained calibration in an LLM agent harness.

### Cited Findings

Theory
- Gneiting and Raftery, "Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation", Journal of the American Statistical Association 102(477):359–378, 2007, DOI 10.1198/016214506000001437, develop the theory of proper scoring rules, under which the expected score is optimized by reporting the true distribution (uniquely for strictly proper rules), with the logarithmic, spherical and quadratic (Brier) scores as examples. Proven. Note that Gneiting and Raftery orient scores so that higher is better, while much later work uses losses. — [search result with bibliographic record](https://colab.ws/articles/10.1198%2F016214506000001437); [DTIC technical report version](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/html/tr/ADA459827/index.html)
- The survey's decision model: if accepting an answer pays a gain when correct and a loss otherwise while rejection pays zero, the expected utility of accepting is linear in a calibrated correctness score, so the optimal policy accepts above a fixed threshold set by the loss-to-total ratio. Calibrated probabilities can therefore be reused across operating points by moving the threshold without retraining. Proven (elementary). — [Li et al. survey PDF](https://yubol-bobo.github.io/rlcd-survey/pdf/calibration-aware-rl-survey.pdf)
- The survey adds that a direct action objective such as TruthRL's is appropriate when one operating cost is stable, while probability reporting suits one model serving several thresholds or changing costs. — [Li et al. survey PDF](https://yubol-bobo.github.io/rlcd-survey/pdf/calibration-aware-rl-survey.pdf)

Learning to defer and selective prediction
- Mozannar and Sontag, "Consistent Estimators for Learning to Defer to an Expert", ICML 2020, arXiv 2006.01862, learn a classifier and a rejector from samples of expert decisions by reducing deferral to cost-sensitive learning with a consistent surrogate loss that generalizes cross-entropy. Proven (consistency) and Empirical. — [arXiv 2006.01862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01862)
- Joshi, Parbhoo, Doshi-Velez, "Learning-to-defer for sequential medical decision-making under uncertainty" (SLTD), arXiv 2109.06312. Per-step deferral ignores long-term effects and timing. SLTD frames deferral as model-based RL, defers when deferring now improves value over waiting a step (accounting for future deferrals), yields pre-emptive deferral, and gives a better trade-off between long-term outcome and deferral frequency on synthetic, semi-synthetic and real data. Empirical. — [arXiv 2109.06312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06312)
- Geifman and El-Yaniv, "Selective Classification for Deep Neural Networks", NeurIPS 2017, arXiv 1705.08500, threshold the maximum softmax response to meet a user-set risk with high probability and describe performance by the risk-coverage curve, reporting a guaranteed 2% top-5 ImageNet error with probability 99.9% at almost 60% coverage. Empirical with a high-probability guarantee. — [arXiv 1705.08500](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500)

Deferral to larger models and to humans in multi-step LLM settings
- Gupta, Narasimhan, Jitkrittum, Rawat, Menon, Kumar, "Language Model Cascades: Token-Level Uncertainty and Beyond", ICLR 2024, arXiv 2404.10136. Extending Chow's probability-based defer rule to generation is not straightforward because sequence-level confidence suffers length bias. A learned post-hoc deferral rule on token-level uncertainty quantiles (and embeddings) beats Chow-Sum and Chow-Average on cost-quality curves (AUC-DF) with FLAN-T5 cascades. Empirical. — [ICLR 2024 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/11f5520daf9132775e8604e89f53925a-Paper-Conference.pdf); [arXiv 2404.10136](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10136v1)
- Ren, Dixit, Bodrova et al., "Robots That Ask For Help: Uncertainty Alignment for Large Language Model Planners" (KnowNo), CoRL 2023 oral, arXiv 2307.01928, uses conformal prediction over an off-the-shelf LLM planner's options to give statistical task-completion guarantees while limiting human help in multi-step planning, without fine-tuning. Proven (coverage guarantee under exchangeability) and Empirical. — [arXiv 2307.01928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01928)
- OpenJev-RLCD motivates its objective exactly as automating confident decisions and escalating the rest, and reports coverage at fixed error (81.1% at 5% error on GSM8K-Verify) as the deferral-relevant metric. — [arXiv 2609.38850](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38850v1)
- Roitman's guide devotes Section 13 to "System 1 + System 2 Cascades", including confidence-based routing, and warns about cascades with correlated errors (pitfall 4 in 17.1); the body text was not read. — [arXiv 2610.33775](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.33775)

Sequential effects on calibration
- open-rlcd reports that Breakout, where outcomes depend on the model's own moves, is the setting where RL calibration beat supervised training (log loss 0.488 vs 0.592, one seed). — [open-rlcd GitHub](https://github.com/Louiskq/open-rlcd)
- decider v10's RL stage scores actions in games with known probability laws and browser click prediction in MiniWoB++, the closest public example of calibration-aware RL in an interactive environment for a Jev-style model. — [decider GitHub](https://github.com/Mapika/decider)
- Maio's critique, reported by dsebastien.net, holds that calibrated individual judgments "do not automatically compose" into calibrated workflows. — [dsebastien.net](https://www.dsebastien.net/reinforcement-learning-for-calibrated-decisions-rlcd/)
- Xuan et al. find that tool type changes agent calibration in multi-turn settings, with evidence tools inducing overconfidence. — [arXiv 2601.07264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07264)

### Inferences
- Properness assumes the event being forecast is fixed. In an agent loop the decision model's actions change the next state, so the per-step "correct action" label is policy-dependent and a per-step proper score no longer certifies calibration of episode outcomes. This is the gap SLTD addresses with value-based deferral and KnowNo addresses with sequence-level conformal coverage.
- Per-step calibration also does not compose: if each step is accepted at confidence 0.95, a ten-step episode succeeds with roughly 0.6 probability only under independence, and errors in agent trajectories are usually correlated. Episode-level evaluation is therefore required in addition to per-step ECE. Heuristic.
- No paper found combines RL-trained calibrated action probabilities with deferral to a larger LLM inside a multi-step agent. Open.

### Gaps
- The full text of Roitman's guide (Sections 13 and 16.8 on RLCD convergence) was not read past 100k characters.
- Work on asking for help in RL agents beyond KnowNo (for example Ask4Help, learning when to ask) and on LLM routers such as RouteLLM or FrugalGPT was not verified in this session.

## 5. Which metrics are standard?

### Takeaway
Report a strictly proper score (Brier and NLL) as the headline, ECE with a reliability diagram as the diagnostic, AUROC for separating correct from incorrect decisions, and risk-coverage (AURC, coverage at fixed error) for the deferral trade-off. Add a cost-quality curve for cascades and episode-level success for the agent.

### Cited Findings
- Guo, Pleiss, Sun, Weinberger, "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks", ICML 2017 (PMLR 70:1321–1330), arXiv 1706.04599, establish modern networks as miscalibrated, define ECE by binning predictions into equal-width bins and comparing mean confidence to accuracy (the scalar summary of a reliability diagram), and show temperature scaling, a one-parameter Platt variant that does not change the argmax, is "surprisingly effective." Empirical. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html)
- systemonemodels.org attributes ECE to Naeini, Cooper and Hauskrecht (2015) and recommends logging probabilities and outcomes on one's own data, bucketing, and comparing each bucket's mean probability to its hit rate. — [systemonemodels.org RLCD guide](https://systemonemodels.org/guides/rlcd-explained/)
- rlcd-lite warns that ECE is biased upward on small samples, with one point estimate outside its own bootstrap interval, and that calibration without resolution is uninformative: its RLCR arm had near-zero resolution and AURC 0.21 against 0.07 for temperature-scaled softmax. — [rlcd-lite GitHub](https://github.com/arnabgho/rlcd-lite)
- Geifman and El-Yaniv define the risk-coverage curve for selective classification. — [arXiv 1705.08500](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500)
- RLCR reports accuracy, AUROC, Brier and ECE. OpenJev-RLCD reports Brier and coverage at fixed error. decider reports accuracy, NLL, Brier, ECE and AURC per task. — [arXiv 2507.16806v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16806v2); [arXiv 2609.38850](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38850v1); [decider GitHub](https://github.com/Mapika/decider)
- Gupta et al. evaluate cascades by the area under the deferral curve (AUC-DF), quality against the fraction deferred to the larger model. — [ICLR 2024 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/11f5520daf9132775e8604e89f53925a-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Roitman's guide covers ECE, the Brier score and the limits of ECE relative to proper scoring rules (9.1), and calibration versus sharpness and reliability diagrams (2.3). — [arXiv 2610.33775](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.33775)
- Kalai et al. and TruthRL motivate reporting hallucination (error) rate and abstention rate separately from accuracy, with all questions as denominator. — [arXiv 2509.04664](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04664v1); [Li et al. survey PDF](https://yubol-bobo.github.io/rlcd-survey/pdf/calibration-aware-rl-survey.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a Jev-style model the survey's warning matters: define which event each reported number is a probability of (the chosen action being correct, or each option being correct) before computing ECE, because TypeSafe's `confidence` statistic is a distribution-shape summary, not a correctness probability.

### Gaps
- Brier (1950) and Naeini et al. (2015) were not fetched directly.

## 6. Which objectives fit the Jev-style harness best, and why?

The setting: at each step a decision model chooses among a fixed list of shell commands, abort, flag danger, or fall back to an LLM, and the team wants its own calibration-aware RL objective.

### Takeaway
Train the action distribution first with a proper scoring rule against labelled actions, then fine-tune with an RLCR-style reward (task utility plus a bounded proper score on the stated probability), and treat fallback-to-LLM as a threshold on the calibrated probability rather than as a learned action. Keep a direct-action ternary reward (TruthRL style, with an explicit fallback cost) as the main baseline, and supervised training plus temperature scaling as the floor that RL has to beat.

### Cited Findings
- A bounded proper score added to a correctness term yields an optimum that is both accurate and calibrated, and the unbounded log score can reward confident errors in this joint setting. — [arXiv 2507.16806v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16806v2)
- Averaging threshold-dependent answer/abstain utilities over thresholds gives correctness-plus-Brier, and calibrated scores let one model serve every deferral threshold, while a direct action objective fits one fixed cost. — [Li et al. survey PDF](https://yubol-bobo.github.io/rlcd-survey/pdf/calibration-aware-rl-survey.pdf)
- Brier on stated confidence without a correctness term collapses accuracy (0.47 to 0.23 in rlcd-lite), RL sharpens the raw softmax towards 1.0, temperature scaling repairs much of this, and a `needs_human` field is not learned at 0.5B. — [rlcd-lite GitHub](https://github.com/arnabgho/rlcd-lite)
- Calibrate-then-reinforce with a KL anchor avoids collapse and policy-gradient noise, using leave-one-out REINFORCE. — [arXiv 2609.38850](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38850v1)
- GRPO's group std normalization induces overconfidence, and PPO, RLOO or GRPO without std normalization stay calibrated. — [arXiv 2508.11800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11800)
- RL beat supervised calibration only where outcomes depended on the model's own moves. — [open-rlcd GitHub](https://github.com/Louiskq/open-rlcd)
- An explicit confidence target t in the prompt with a t/(1−t) penalty makes "answer iff confidence exceeds t" optimal for every t. — [arXiv 2509.04664](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04664v1)
- Abstention as a learned action can collapse under error-penalized rewards when training success is low. — [Li et al. survey PDF, citing arXiv 2608.00301](https://yubol-bobo.github.io/rlcd-survey/pdf/calibration-aware-rl-survey.pdf)
- Sequential deferral should compare deferring now against later (SLTD), and conformal prediction gives multi-step help guarantees (KnowNo). — [arXiv 2109.06312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06312); [arXiv 2307.01928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01928)

### Inferences
- Recommended objective, stage 1 (supervised, proper score). Fit the softmax over shell commands and abort with log loss against oracle or teacher-LLM labels on recorded harness states, then fit one temperature on held-out states. This is RLCD's stated goal reached by the cheapest route, and the open reproductions show it is hard to beat when labels exist.
- Stage 2 (RL, RLCR-style). Roll out the agent, sample the action, and reward each step with a utility term from the outcome (command succeeded and advanced the task, or the episode's terminal success propagated back) plus a bounded proper score (Brier) on the probability the model assigned to the action it took. Anchor with KL to the stage-1 model, and use RLOO or GRPO without std normalization. This is where RL adds something, because the next state depends on the chosen command.
- Fallback-to-LLM as a threshold, not an action. Defer when the calibrated top-action probability falls below a threshold set from the LLM's cost relative to an error's cost (the survey's linear-utility rule). The threshold can then be retuned when the LLM's price or latency changes, and the risk-coverage curve becomes the evaluation. Training fallback as a softmax option instead learns one operating point and risks abstention collapse.
- Flag danger as a separate binary head. Danger is not mutually exclusive with a command choice and its costs are asymmetric, so a separate yes/no probability trained with Brier or log loss and thresholded with a low, cost-weighted threshold fits better than one more softmax slot. Heuristic.
- Abort as a regular action with an explicit negative utility, so the reward does not make abort the safe default (Kalai's guessing argument run in reverse).
- Optional variant for one policy across thresholds. Put a confidence target t into the state and use Kalai's t/(1−t) penalty for wrong actions, so the policy learns behavioral calibration for every deferral threshold, which can be compared against post-hoc thresholding of the stage-2 probabilities.
- Baselines to report: supervised plus temperature scaling (floor), binary-reward RL (shows the overconfidence RLCR fixes), ternary direct-action RL with a fixed fallback cost (TruthRL analogue), and RLCR-style RL. Metrics: per-step NLL, Brier, ECE with reliability diagram and bootstrap intervals, AUROC, AURC and coverage at fixed error, a cost-success curve over the fallback threshold (AUC-DF analogue), and episode-level success and danger recall, since per-step calibration does not compose.

### Gaps
- No published system trains calibrated action probabilities for a shell-command agent with LLM fallback. The recommendation combines results from single-step QA, games and text-to-SQL agents, and its transfer to shell harnesses is Open.
- How to assign per-step credit for terminal episode success without destroying per-step calibration has no tested answer in the sources found. SLTD's value-based deferral is the nearest model.
